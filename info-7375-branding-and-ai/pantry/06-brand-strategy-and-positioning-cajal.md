## FIGURE 1 · Brand strategy vs. marketing strategy · Important · comparison panels
The distinction most people skip. Two panels: brand strategy answers "who are we, for whom, what will we never compromise?" (the constraint set); marketing strategy answers "how do we reach our audience?" (channels, messages, funnels). An arrow shows marketing is downstream of brand.
- Spatial: two side-by-side panels with the defining question and 2–3 contents each; a downstream arrow from brand → marketing beneath them.
- RED: none — two peer concepts being separated; the arrow carries the dependency without color.
- EXCLUSIONS: no seven-component list (that is Fig 2), no Stripe specifics, no negative space.

## FIGURE 2 · The seven brand components · Critical · comparison table
The spine of the chapter. Rows: Mission, Vision, Values, UVP, Archetype, Voice, Positioning; columns: the job only it does, the Stripe example, what breaks without it. Each component constrains the others.
- Spatial: seven-row table, three labeled columns; row headers left; compact one-line cells.
- RED: none — seven peer components, each doing a distinct job; a neutral reference matrix.
- EXCLUSIONS: no dependency-chain arrows (that is Fig 3), no negative-space list, no naming tests.

## FIGURE 3 · The dependency chain · Critical · systems diagram
How the seven components constrain one another, with negative space as the downstream consequence. Mission → scopes Vision; Values → bound what's done to reach it; UVP → consistent with values; Archetype → governs expression; Voice → encodes audience+archetype; Positioning → names the real alternative. Negative space sits at the bottom as the product of all seven.
- Spatial: seven components stacked vertically with downward dependency arrows; a "Negative Space" node at the base receiving from the stack; short edge labels on the constraint relationships.
- RED: the "Negative Space" node — the downstream output the chapter argues is what actually makes a brand legible, the focal payoff.
- EXCLUSIONS: no Stripe example cells (that is Fig 2), no naming content, no coherence-ring (that is Fig 7).

## FIGURE 4 · The Stripe inversion · Critical · comparison panels
The mechanism: a brand is defined by what it declines. Two columns of equal weight — what Stripe built (clean API, developer-first, global) on the left; what Stripe declined (enterprise sales, broad SMB marketing, rapid product proliferation, competitor-bashing, premature launches) on the right. Both load-bearing; the refusals are the brand.
- Spatial: two equal columns under a shared header, "built" left | "declined" right, four to five aligned items per side; a footer line "the pattern of refusals is the brand."
- RED: the "declined" column header — the chapter's central inversion, the side the reader is told carries the brand.
- EXCLUSIONS: no negative-space criteria (that is Fig 5), no archetype-audience content, no dependency chain.

## FIGURE 5 · Negative-space criteria · Important · comparison table
The four tests every no-list item must pass: Specific, Archetype-consistent, Costly, Testable over time. Columns: what passes, what fails, a Stripe example. Turns a vague refusal into a real commitment.
- Spatial: four-row table (one criterion each), three labeled columns; pass/fail cells rendered as short contrasting phrases.
- RED: none — four peer criteria a no-list item must all satisfy; neutral reference.
- EXCLUSIONS: no built-vs-declined columns (that is Fig 4), no naming tests, no audience-archetype grid.

## FIGURE 6 · Audience types and archetype fit · Important · comparison table
Picking the role the audience needs. Rows: developers, first-time founders in hostile markets, buyers in incumbent-dominated markets; columns: what they fear, want, distrust, reward, and the archetype fit (Sage / Caregiver / Rebel). The reward structure points to the archetype.
- Spatial: three-row table, five labeled columns; the final "archetype fit" column set off as the conclusion of each row.
- RED: the "archetype fit" column — the output of the four-question diagnosis, what each row resolves to.
- EXCLUSIONS: no shadow content, no naming tests, no seven-component table.

## FIGURE 7 · The circular coherence check · Critical · cycle diagram
The seven components arranged in a ring, each adjacent pair joined by an arrow labeled with its consistency question (Mission↔UVP, Values↔Negative Space, Archetype↔Voice, Positioning↔UVP, and so on). A contradiction on any edge means the document defers the conflict to a worse moment.
- Spatial: seven nodes on a ring, curved arrows between adjacent pairs, each arrow carrying its check question; a center label "internal coherence."
- RED: none — every adjacent check is a peer; the ring's completeness is the point, not one edge.
- EXCLUSIONS: no Stripe example cells, no naming content, no built/declined columns.

## FIGURE 8 · Archetype naming patterns · Supplementary · comparison table
Names as archetype signals. Rows for six archetypes (Sage, Hero, Caregiver, Magician, Rebel, Creator); columns: naming tendency, examples in the wild (Stripe/Linear; Salesforce/Cloudflare; Calm/Headspace; Palantir/Anthropic; etc.), connotation to avoid. Shows a name that fights its archetype fights itself.
- Spatial: six-row table, three labeled columns; the "examples" column listing 2–3 real names per archetype.
- RED: none — six peer naming families; a neutral reference table.
