# Professor's Handbook: Creating an AI Textbook with Claude Code

*A practical guide for non-technical professors, written from experience co-authoring INFO 7375 at Northeastern University.*

---

## Step 1: Get Access to Claude Code

Before you can do anything, you need the right software license. At Northeastern, this means going through ITS — not your department or COE IT directly (they'll point you there anyway).

**What to request:** The **Premium version of Claude**, which includes **Claude Code**.

### How to request access

**Option A — Submit a ticket online:**
Visit the ITS Service Portal and open a ticket requesting the Premium Claude / Claude Code license.
[Submit an ITS Ticket](https://northeastern.edu) *(link: ITS Ticket)*

**Option B — Email ITS directly:**
Send a request to [help@northeastern.edu](mailto:help@northeastern.edu) and ask for the Premium version of Claude, which contains Claude Code.

### Tips for your request
- Mention your course name and number (e.g., INFO 7375)
- Explain that you'll be using Claude Code for course content development
- CC your department administrator if helpful for approval
- **It may take a few days** for ITS to respond — plan ahead before your project start date

---

## Step 2: Set Up Your Project Folder

Once you have access and are ready to start, create a folder on your computer for your textbook. A few important rules:

### Don't move your folder once you start

Once you begin working with Claude Code, **do not move, rename, or reorganize your project folder**. Claude Code builds internal links based on the folder's location. If you move the folder, those links break and you may lose your session context.

**Best practice:** Decide where your folder will live before you start (e.g., your Desktop or Documents) and leave it there.
