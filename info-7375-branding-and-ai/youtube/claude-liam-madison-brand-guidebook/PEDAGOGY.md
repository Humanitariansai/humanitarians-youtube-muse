# PEDAGOGY AUDIT — claude-liam-madison-brand-guidebook

## Concept chain (B00–B39, ~6:40 estimated)

**Bookend**
- (B00) ASK: Liam has a 32-page InDesign template. He wants to adapt it for his brand. He's never opened InDesign. Claude says yes. Walkthrough promised.

**ACT I — What You Downloaded (B01–B06)**
1. (B01) Act card.
2. (B02) Cover page still. Thirty-two pages, 16:9 presentation format. Someone else's name — that's the point of a template: a brand with the brand left blank.
3. (B03) Three-file distinction: .indt (master), .idml (the file Claude can edit), .pdf (look only). Three formats, three jobs.
4. (B04) Contents page still. Eight sections laid out as a roadmap — brand, logo, color, type, online, stationery, imagery, info. Everything a brand might design for.
5. (B05) Isotype grid: 32 tiles colored by section. A brand is a small system of repeated decisions, not a logo with accessories.
6. (B06) Delete pages is the first act of branding. The template overshoots on purpose — cutting what you don't need IS the customization.

**ACT II — The Logo System (B07–B13)**
7. (B07) Act card.
8. (B08) Welcome page still. Your name, title, skills in the stock persona's slot. One text swap — deferred to the adaptation act.
9. (B09) R1a: logo page tight on elements key. The mark, tagline slot, spacing. Purpose of the page: teach whoever inherits the brand how not to wreck it.
10. (B10) R1b: zoom out — primary mark in full context. A logo isn't an image. It's an image plus rules.
11. (B11) Clear space rule drawn on. Margin defined by the mark's own proportions; scales wherever the logo goes.
12. (B12) Two marks, two jobs: full signature for hero surfaces, BB monogram for tight corners/avatars. Size thresholds define which goes where.
13. (B13) The don'ts: stretch, recolor, crowd. Three ways brands die. Documented once so you never have to say it again.

**ACT III — Color & Type (B14–B21)**
14. (B14) Act card.
15. (B15) Color page still. Four swatch cards, tint ladders — the Bear Brown browns, pulled from the brand's site code not a mood board.
16. (B16) Tint system: 40/60/80/100 per color. The ladder turns one color into a palette instead of a paint chip.
17. (B17) The adaptation demo: template orange (#FF7929) family morphs to Bear Brown brown (#8B3A0F) family across all swatches. Not hex-swap — the whole color family re-keyed. One instruction to Claude.
18. (B18) 3:1 rule: decoration colors vs meaning colors. If a color carries meaning it clears 3:1 contrast; if it can't, it decorates. Accessibility as a brand decision.
19. (B19) Typography specimen still. Primary typeface at 60pt — uppercase, lowercase, numerals. If type only works inside the logo, you have lettering, not a typeface.
20. (B20) Pairing rule: display face for headlines, workhorse for everything else. Roles labeled so nobody invents a third font later.
21. (B21) Triple-typeface page still. Three sizes, three weights. Boring on purpose — consistency is what typography is for.

**ACT IV — Online & Stationery (B22–B29)**
22. (B22) Act card.
23. (B23) Online post page still. Post styles, media kit. A social post is a designed surface, not an afterthought.
24. (B24) Social crops: one mark in avatar / banner / story. Where the monogram earns its keep — the full signature dies at 64px. BB doesn't.
25. (B25) R2a: stationery tight on business card. Monogram, brown, cream type. Nothing on it that color and logo sections didn't already decide.
26. (B26) R2b: zoom out — card, letterhead, envelope as one system. A brand stops being a website and becomes an object on someone's desk.
27. (B27) The mockups are live InDesign frames, not screenshots. Swap the mark once, every surface updates. Template vs picture of a template.
28. (B28) Isotype count: 3 surfaces, 1 mark, 1 brown, 0 new decisions. Many surfaces, few decisions — that's the entire argument for a guidebook.
29. (B29) Spacing note from inside the template: "spacing can be reduced based on design type." A guidebook that admits its rules bend is one people actually follow.

**ACT V — Adapt It With Claude (B30–B36)**
30. (B30) Act card.
31. (B31) Imagery page still. Placeholder: place product or brand images here. Your photos, your treatment.
32. (B32) Contact page still. Real Bear Brown details filled — one line of your details, Claude fills every field.
33. (B33) IDML = zip of XML. Unzip: designmap.xml, Stories/ (all text), Resources/Graphic.xml (colors). Claude reads it, edits it, re-zips it. No InDesign in that loop.
34. (B34) Three-step workflow: Claude edits IDML → you double-click (InDesign opens) → File, Export, PDF. You never learn InDesign. It's just the print button.
35. (B35) The prompt is public. Six brackets: name, website, email, title, address, accent color. Lives on the download page. One paste.
36. (B36) Before/after: stock name, stock orange, stock mark → your name, your hex, your logo. Same 32 pages. Zero design hours.

**Closing block (B37–B39)**
37. (B37) Verdict artifact: 32 pages · 8 sections, 3 files, 1 prompt, InDesign as export.
38. (B38) YOUR TURN: Liam reads the full adapt-it prompt; directs to madison.humanitarians.ai/brand-guidebook.

## Conceptual prerequisites (viewer)
- What a brand guidebook is
- Familiarity with the idea that design files have formats (.psd, .pdf, etc.)
- No InDesign knowledge assumed — that's the premise

## Pedagogy checks
- **Concrete before abstract**: cover page and contents shown (B02–B04) before the system diagram (B05).
- **Problem stated before solution**: B03 (three formats) names the puzzle before the workflow payoff at B33–B34.
- **Adapt demo is the teach**: the Bear Brown rebrand (B17, B32) is the worked example, not an aside.
- **Your Turn reads the actual prompt**: B38 reads the six-bracket template verbatim, directing to the download page — viewers can act immediately.
- **ASK lands answered in B00**: the intro states the problem and promises the answer; both delivered by B39.

## Exclusions enforced
- No InDesign UI tutorial — the point is explicitly that viewers never open InDesign.
- No extended IDML XML walkthrough — one beat, structural map only.
- No typography theory — specimen pages shown, not explained from first principles.
- No color-theory lecture — tint ladders shown as a system, 3:1 as a brand decision, not a WCAG deep-dive.
