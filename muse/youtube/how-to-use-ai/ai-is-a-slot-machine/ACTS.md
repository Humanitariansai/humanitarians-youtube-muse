# ACTS.md — AI is a slot machine.

A show-tell concept film for the humanitarians AI YouTube channel: why
everyone reacts to AI the way they do, and the one mental model that fixes
it — AI is a slot machine. Probabilistic, not deterministic. Pull the
lever many times; keep the best.

**Exact title:** AI is a slot machine.

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer understands what "probabilistic"
means in plain language, recognizes which of the five stages of AI use
they're stuck in, and has the gambler's playbook: ask for three versions,
manage the AI like an intern, think in batches.

**Structure:** Three acts.

- Act 1 — The machine (4 beats): the slot machine arrives; the same prompt
  gives different answers (probabilistic, model defined in the same breath);
  the five-stage pattern, borrowed from the Kübler-Ross stages of grief.
- Act 2 — The four stuck stages (4 beats): denial (one bad answer, told ever
  since — or blind trust); anger (screenshotting bad outputs); bargaining
  (the perfect-prompt fallacy, where most people are stuck); depression
  (six hours, nothing to show, quitting).
- Act 3 — The gambler and the playbook (3 beats): acceptance — pull a
  hundred times, keep five, trash the rest; rule one (ask for three
  versions); rule two (manage it like an intern — "ask me clarifying
  questions"); rule three (think in batches).

**Tone:** Teardown at its plainest — short sentences, every term defined in
the same breath it appears, no jargon left standing. Liam's voice, warm and
direct.

**What this film is not:** not a grief-psychology lesson — Kübler-Ross is a
borrowed pattern, named as such, not a clinical claim about AI users. Not a
prompt-engineering course — the film argues the perfect prompt doesn't
exist. Not a product demo — no specific AI product is shown or named beyond
the handoff's "paste this into Claude."

**Source facts:**
- Kübler-Ross five stages (denial, anger, bargaining, depression,
  acceptance — DABDA) introduced in the 1969 book *On Death and Dying*,
  from interviews with terminally ill patients; not a fixed sequence and
  not everyone experiences all five. (psychology/ medical sources)
  [record]
- LLMs sample from a next-token probability distribution, so the same
  prompt can produce different outputs across runs; not fully
  deterministic even at temperature 0. (Anthropic API docs via
  engineering coverage, 2025) [record]
- The five-stage argument, the "AI gambler," and the stage-exit moves
  (three versions, "ask me clarifying questions," batches) are the source
  beat sheet's structure and judgments, rewritten for a general audience.
  [record]

**Skill:** show-tell. One drawing per beat; the slot machine is the cast
object in every body beat; minimal on-screen labels, all read aloud.
