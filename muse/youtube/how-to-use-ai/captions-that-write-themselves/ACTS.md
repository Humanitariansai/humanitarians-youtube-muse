# ACTS.md — Captions That Write Themselves

Film 40 of the humanitarians channel "How to AI" series (how-to-ai lane,
Wave 6 "Making things"). A practical how-to: AI-generated subtitles and
captions for a normal person's phone-shot video — accessibility and
reach, without the typing.

**Exact title:** Captions That Write Themselves

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer knows what captions are
(words on screen, timed to the sound), how the AI makes them (one pass:
listen, write, time), the four steps to do it tonight (open a captioning
tool, press the captions button, check names and odd words, choose
burned-in or a caption file), why it matters (most phone video is
watched with the sound off; hundreds of millions of viewers cannot hear
the audio at all), and gets a copy-paste prompt for an AI caption-draft
check to run this week.

**Structure:** Hook + show-tell body + closing.

- Hook (2 beats): BIDEA — the naive job corrected ("type out the
  captions" → "let the AI write the captions"); BDEFS — three plain
  terms (captions / subtitles / auto-captioning).
- Body (8 beats): B00 the hero object — your phone-shot video arrives;
  B01 what captions are — lines landing on the frame, timed to the
  speech; B02 the one pass — the AI listens, writes, times; B03 step
  one — open a captioning tool; B04 step two — press the button, the
  draft writes itself; B05 step three — the one human job: fix names
  and odd words; B06 step four — burned in or a caption file; B07 why
  it matters — the attributed numbers (sound-off viewing, hearing
  loss), bars plus a hero number.
- Closing (2 beats): BHTF your-turn composer with the full copy-paste
  caption-check prompt; BOUT spoken outro.

**Source argument:** NEW — built from scratch, no mirror source. The
argument is the film's own: captioning is one of the highest-return,
lowest-effort improvements to any video, and the AI now does the boring
part in a pass, leaving one human job — the names.

**What this film is not:** not a tool review (no named app is
recommended or ranked; the steps are tool-agnostic on purpose); not a
claim about any product's accuracy (the film says "nearly right" and
sends the viewer to check); not a pricing comparison (no pricing tiers
quoted anywhere, per the standing rule — the decision framework is
burned-in for feeds vs a caption file for YouTube); not a statistics
film (the two numbers used are both attributed aloud and captioned on
screen, per the thin-numbers law).

**Tone:** plain and direct; Teardown register throughout. The voice
explains, the drawings show. One idea per beat, drawn once. Every
technical term is defined in the same breath it is introduced —
"auto-captioning: an AI listens to your video and writes the captions
for you, line by line."

**Skill:** show-tell (one drawing per beat, minimal labels, the voice
explains; bookends: hesitant writer, key terms, Your Turn composer,
spoken @NikBearBrown outro). Zero cards: every body beat is a drawing
of the film's own cast (the phone video, caption lines, the dark AI
block, the check stamps). The B07 number beat is a drawing too — bars
and a hero number carry the attributed figures better than a card could.
