# ACTS.md — Claude, Your Quizmaster

Film 2 of the humanitarians channel Muse series (how-to-use-ai lane). A
practical how-to: stop asking Claude for answers and start making it ask
you questions — four lab-backed moves that turn the chat window into a
quizmaster.

**Exact title:** Claude, Your Quizmaster.

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer knows four concrete moves —
quiz-don't-ask, predict-before-you-look, spaced reviews, explain-it-back —
can run each of them inside Claude tonight, and gets a copy-paste prompt
that does it.

**Structure:** Hook + two acts + closing.

- Hook (2 beats): BIDEA — the naive ask corrected ("explain this chapter
  to me" → "quiz me on this chapter"); BDEFS — four plain terms.
- Act 1 — The four moves (4 beats): B01 why quizzing works (the testing
  effect, 61 vs 40); B02 predict-first, performed live on the viewer;
  B03 spaced repetition (today / tomorrow / next week / next month);
  B04 explain-it-back (Claude grades your explanation; the gaps are the
  study plan).
- Act 2 — The moves at work (1 beat): B05 the same four moves applied to
  a new regulation, a new platform, a new product line — Claude handles
  repetition, you bring the judgment about what matters.
- Closing (3 beats): BVDT recap of both acts; BHTF your-turn composer
  with the full copy-paste quiz prompt; BOUT spoken outro.

**Source argument (from the mirror beat sheet):** "The best use of an
answer machine is making it ask the questions." Preserved beat for beat:
the four moves, the live predict-before-reveal performed on the viewer,
the recap's "the struggle is the learning."

**What changed for the general audience:** the "Claude, For Students"
framing is gone. The school examples survive only where they are the
clearest illustration; the film's home examples are work life — a
compliance rule, a platform rollout, a client pitch. Every technical term
is defined in the same breath it is introduced. No term survives
unexplained.

**Tone:** plain and direct; Teardown register throughout. The voice
explains, the drawings show. No jokes, no setup-and-payoff — one idea
per beat, drawn once.

**What this film is not:** not a product tour of Claude; not a claim
that Claude is a tutor or knows the viewer's field; not a citation of
exact retention percentages as promises (the 61/40 figure is a lab
result, labeled as such). It does not claim quizzing replaces learning
the material first — you quiz what you have already met.

**Skill:** show-tell (one drawing per beat, minimal labels, the voice
explains; bookends: hesitant writer, key terms, Your Turn composer,
spoken @NikBearBrown outro).
