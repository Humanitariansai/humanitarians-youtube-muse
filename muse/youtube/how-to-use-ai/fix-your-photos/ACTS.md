## The argument (one paragraph)

You've got a photo you love, except for one thing in it. The AI tools in your
photo app can change the photo itself: erase the photobomber (Google Photos
calls its eraser Magic Eraser), and the AI paints in the background it
expects — grass, fence, sky. That painting-in is hallucination: the AI
inventing a detail it can't see, fine on grass and sky, risky on faces, hands,
and words on signs. Then fix the lighting with the app's one-tap suggestion
(in Google Photos it's called Enhance): brighter, warmer, better colors,
without hunting the slider forest. Before the fancy edits, straighten the
horizon and tighten the frame. Three rules: edit a copy, never the original;
zoom into the spot the AI fixed and look at the edges before you keep it; and
an edited photo is a made thing — the album and the print shop are fine, but
never show it as what really happened, and never edit documents. Then print
the one finished file.

## Deliberate anti-rot choices

- The edits are framed as "the AI tools in your photo app", with Google
  Photos' Magic Eraser and Enhance named as the verified example — never a
  pixel position, never a version number, never a product-specific flow.
- Claude is used only to plan (attach + ask what to change), matching its
  verified see-and-describe ability from the companion film's FACTCHECK — the
  film makes NO claim that Claude itself erases or edits photos.
- No pricing, no model names (beyond the house `modelLabel: "Opus 5.5"` composer
  chrome shared with the companion film), no availability claims in narration.
- "Hallucination" is taught as the plain-language name for the AI inventing
  the fill, not as a technical spec of any editing model.
- B08's honesty beat asserts no vendor policy; it teaches the durable rule
  (a made thing is fine for the album, never as evidence, never documents).
