# ACTS.md — Learn Anything Faster

Film 13 of the humanitarians channel "How to AI" series (how-to-ai lane).
A practical how-to: stop asking the AI for answers and start asking it to
teach you — three concrete moves (explain-it-simple, one-at-a-time quiz,
Socratic mode) demonstrated on one concrete topic: how a mortgage works.

**Exact title:** Learn Anything Faster

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer knows three moves — (1) "explain
it like I'm smart but new to it", (2) "quiz me, one question at a time,
and don't move on until I get it", (3) "don't give me the answer, ask me
leading questions" — has seen all three performed on how mortgages work,
and gets a copy-paste tutor prompt to run tonight on their own topic.

**Structure:** Hook + show-tell body + closing.

- Hook (2 beats): BIDEA — the naive ask corrected ("Give me the answer
  about mortgages" → "Teach me like a tutor about mortgages"); BDEFS —
  three plain terms (tutor mode / quiz mode / Socratic mode).
- Body (5 beats): B00 the hero object — a house, the demo topic (how a
  mortgage works); B01 move one, the house opens into three blocks
  (borrow / interest / pay back); B02 move two, the tutor quizzes one
  question at a time and waits; B03 move three, Socratic mode on the
  question "why does a little extra each month save so much?"; B04 the
  transfer — the house was the demo, the moves work on any topic.
- Closing (2 beats): BHTF your-turn composer with the full copy-paste
  tutor prompt; BOUT spoken outro.

**Source argument:** NEW — built from scratch, no mirror source. The
argument is the film's own: the AI is the most patient tutor ever, and
the three moves are how you switch it from answer machine to teacher.

**What this film is not:** not a claim that the AI is always right (the
film never says "the AI will correctly…"); not a mortgage advice film
(the mortgage is only the demo topic — no rates, no recommendations);
not a citation of retention statistics (no percentages are promised to
the viewer). It does not claim quizzing or Socratic mode replaces first
meeting the material — the moves run on a topic you have already met.

**Tone:** plain and direct; Teardown register throughout. The voice
explains, the drawings show. One idea per beat, drawn once. Every
technical term is defined in the same breath it is introduced —
"Socratic mode" is named for Socrates, the Greek philosopher who taught
by asking questions.

**Skill:** show-tell (one drawing per beat, minimal labels, the voice
explains; bookends: hesitant writer, key terms, Your Turn composer,
spoken @NikBearBrown outro). Zero cards: every body beat is a drawing
of the film's own cast (the house, the tutor card, the learner, the
question pills).
