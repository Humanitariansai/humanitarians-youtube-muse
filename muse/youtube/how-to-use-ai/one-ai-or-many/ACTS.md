**Exact title:** One AI or many?

**Core promise:** By the end, a viewer stops worrying about which AI tool
is "best" and knows the simple decision rule instead: the differences
between top tools are smaller than the difference between using one well
and using one badly — so pick one, learn it properly (this series teaches
you how), and add a second only when you hit a specific, named wall
(image-generation strength, coding strength, ecosystem lock-in, price).

**Anti-obsession beat (the spine's load-bearing idea):** the leaderboard
changes monthly; your skill transfers. No version numbers, no rankings, no
prices anywhere — every comparison is generic and framework-level, so the
film stays true when the leaderboard turns over.

**Structure:** three acts.

- Act 1 — The question, reframed (4 beats: B00–B03): cold open on the
  shopper's question; BLUF (the trick isn't picking the best AI, it's
  learning to use one well); four terms in plain English (AI tool, prompt,
  wall, leaderboard); the two gaps (tools differ little, users differ
  hugely).
- Act 2 — Why not to chase (4 beats: B04–B07): the leaderboard's shelf
  life; skill transfers; the decision rule (pick one you have, learn it —
  this series teaches how); the four walls that justify a second tool.
- Act 3 — The cost and the verdict (4 beats: B08, BVDT, BHTF, BOUT): the
  hidden cost of tool-shopping, with a nod to the companion film's stack
  verdict; the verdict (five lines); your turn (a paste-into-Claude
  interview prompt that names the wall); title outro.
