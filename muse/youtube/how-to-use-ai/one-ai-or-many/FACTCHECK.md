# FACTCHECK.md — "One AI or many?"

Checked 2026-10-03. This film is a NEW build (no mirror source), and its
whole design rule is: no version numbers, no rankings, no prices, no
timeless capability claims. Every comparison is generic and
framework-level. Verdicts: SUPPORTED / QUALIFY / UNSUPPORTED / DROPPED;
editorial judgments labeled as such.

## 1. No version numbers, model names, rankings, or prices anywhere — editorial design rule

The build brief forbids asserting specific model versions, rankings, or
prices as timeless facts. This film names NO vendors at all (unlike the
companion film, which names Claude and ChatGPT): it speaks of "AI tools"
generically. The podium in M05 carries generic icons, never names. The
thirty-minute test and the companion's verdict are referenced as the
companion's own argument, not re-litigated. [my input]

## 2. "Leaderboards change monthly" — characterization, QUALIFY

The film's phrasing is deliberately loose: tools "trade places every few
weeks — new releases, new scores, new king". This is a characterization of
the fast-churning AI release cycle, not a claim that a specific benchmark
reorders on a specific schedule. The companion film's SOURCES.md documents
the churn concretely (GPT-5.5 announced April 2026, retired October 2026;
eight Claude releases in twelve months) — that evidence sits behind this
film's generic phrasing, which is kept version-free so the film does not
date itself. [judgment]

## 3. "The differences between top AI tools are smaller than the gap between good and bad use" — editorial/methodological

The film's central claim (B01, B03, verdict) is a judgment about where a
user's effort pays off, not a factual assertion about tool capabilities.
M04's bars are deliberately unlabeled by number — they illustrate a
relationship the narration frames as opinion in Teardown register. No
benchmark figures are cited because none are needed for the claim as
framed. [my input]

## 4. "Your skill transfers" — editorial/methodological

Prompt craft, judging drafts, iterating with follow-ups, and knowing when
to stop are user-side practices, not product features; the film presents
them as portable across tools. This is advice grounded in how the tools
work (all major chat AI products accept natural-language prompts and
produce drafts to judge), not a benchmark claim. [my input]

## 5. The four walls (image strength, coding strength, ecosystem lock-in, price) — editorial

The four categories that justify a second tool are presented as a
decision framework, not as a survey of which tool leads where. Deliberately,
the film does NOT name which tool is stronger at images or coding —
that would be a version-dated claim. "Your files and team already live in
another tool's world" is ecosystem lock-in stated as a user's own
situation, not a product comparison. [my input]

## 6. Consistency with the companion film — checked, no contradiction

`everyone-wants-one-ai` ("ChatGPT or Claude?") argues: the one rule (use
what you have, or both), benchmarks don't decide, the 30-minute test, and
"one AI is a fantasy — the stack is the answer." This film argues: pick one
first, learn it well, and only then add a second at a named wall —
"build the stack slowly". Both agree the leaderboard is not the decider;
both agree a second tool is legitimate (the companion says "get both";
this film says "add a second at a wall"). The companion's test is cited as
the viewer's tie-breaker (B08, verdict), strengthening rather than
repeating it. No contradiction. [record]

## 7. "This series teaches you how" — forward reference, SUPPORTED

B06 points the viewer at the series' own how-to films for learning one tool
well (asking well, showing examples, checking drafts). The series folder
`muse/youtube/how-to-use-ai/` contains films on exactly these practices.
A forward reference to a series that exists. [record]

## 8. No invented statistics, no sourced claims left unsourced

Nothing in the narration is a number that requires a source: "thirty
minutes" is the companion film's own test; "twelve" tools and "a hundred
hours" are rhetorical illustration, not survey data. No statistic is
asserted as measured. [record]
