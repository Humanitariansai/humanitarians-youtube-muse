# ACTS.md — Your personal research assistant

A general-audience show-tell film (humanitarians channel Muse series).
Deep-research features: how to brief the AI well, how to read the
report it brings back, and what to distrust in it. Companion to Film 21,
"Trust, but verify" (which teaches the 60-second checking habit; this
film teaches the briefing and reading habits around a deep-research
report). NEW — built from scratch, no mirror source.

**Exact title:** Your personal research assistant

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer can brief a deep-research AI
like a research assistant and read what it brings back with open eyes:
(1) start the brief with the decision, not the topic; (2) set the scope
— where to look, what to skip, which dates; (3) demand the answer's
shape — a citation on every claim, unknowns flagged; then when the
report lands: (4) read it in layers, sources list before claims;
(5) spot-check two citations; and distrust (6) shiny reports with hollow
citations, (7) citation echo loops, (8) stale dates and single-source
answers. Plus the closing rule: the report is a map, not the answer —
you stay the editor.

**Structure:** Three acts, 13 beats, ~240 s (~4 min).

- Act 1 — The hook and the vocabulary (2 beats): the hesitant writer
  types "Can you do all my research for my report?" and corrects it to
  "brief my research assistant" — the film's whole move in one
  correction. Four plain-language terms: deep research, brief,
  citation, primary source.
- Act 2 — Brief it, then read it (8 beats): B00 shows the hero — the
  research desk that hunts dozens of sources and returns a cited
  report. B01–B03 build the three-line brief (decision, scope,
  demanded shape). B04–B05 teach reading (layers; spot-check two
  citations). B06–B08 teach distrust (hollow citations, the echo loop,
  stale dates / single-source answers).
- Act 3 — The rule and handoff (3 beats): the report is a map, not
  the answer; a paste-ready briefing prompt in the Your Turn composer
  (read in full), with two self-checks; the title-restate outro.

**Tone:** Teardown register — plain sentences, every term explained in
the same breath, show rather than tell. The AI is a tireless assistant
with no judgment; the viewer stays the editor. No product tour, no
version numbers, no prices: the advice is durable across tools.

**What this film is not:** not a review or ranking of deep-research
products (no vendor is named as best); not a how-to-click tutorial
(no menu paths, which churn); not research advice for any specific
medical, legal, or financial decision. It teaches *how to brief and
read*, never what any answer is.

**Source facts (search-grounded 2026-10-04, see SOURCES.md):**
- Deep-research modes exist across the major chat AIs (ChatGPT Deep
  Research, Claude Research, Gemini Deep Research, Perplexity Deep
  Research): they browse the web, read many sources, and return cited
  reports, taking minutes (roughly 5–30) rather than seconds.
- AI-generated misinformation can enter retrieval corpora and get
  cited back by AI systems — Lily Ray's documented "AI slop loop"
  (ppc.land, 2026-04-14): a real feedback cycle behind the film's
  "echo" beat.
- The Tow Center for Digital Journalism found generative search tools
  frequently provide incorrect or incomplete citations without
  disclaiming uncertainty — behind "demand unknowns flagged" and
  "hollow citations".
- A Stanford study found AI chatbots draw on a much narrower set of
  sources than a plain web search — behind "thin research" (stale /
  single-source).
- LLM confabulation (fluent confident falsehoods, invented citations)
  is standard documented behavior — carried over from Film 21's
  FACTCHECK.
