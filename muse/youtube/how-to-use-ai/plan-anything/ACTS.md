## The argument

Planning is where AI shines, because it never gets tired of revising — so you
plan *with* it, step by step, instead of asking for one finished plan. The film
demonstrates the full arc on a weekend trip (constraints first, then a draft,
then a real revision — "too rushed, slow it down" — then a packing checklist),
then shows the same loop working for projects and budgets, and closes with the
one rule that never changes: the machine plans, you verify.

Source: NEW — built from scratch, no mirror source. The trip is a fictional
worked example (the "hundred and fifty dollars" is the persona's demo
constraint, not a price claim); the constraints → draft → revise → checklist
loop is the film's own pedagogical construct.

## Why this act order

The question (BIDEA) corrects the one-shot habit into the partnership the
film teaches; the terms (BDEFS) load the three words the loop needs; the hero
(B00) shows the loop as one picture before any step runs. B01–B04 then run the
trip arc in loop order — you can't draft before you have constraints, and you
can't revise before you have a draft. B05–B06 generalize the pattern to two
more domains (kept as two beats because a project timeline and a money split
are different pictures, not one). B07 closes the body with the verify rule —
the one line the brief mandates — placed last so it's the last thing the
viewer hears before Your Turn. No recap beat: the loop blocks return in B05
and B06, so nothing needs restating.
