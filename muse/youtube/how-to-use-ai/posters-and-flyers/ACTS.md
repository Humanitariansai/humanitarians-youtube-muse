# ACTS.md — Posters and Flyers

Film 38 of the humanitarians-channel "How to AI" series (Wave 6, "Making
things"). A practical how-to for non-designers: image generation applied to
real-world printables — a garage sale, a bake sale, a side business. This
film teaches the PRINTABLE WORKFLOW; the companion film
`pictures-from-words` (film 19) teaches the image-generation ladder and the
five-slot prompt recipe. This film references it and does not re-teach it.

**Exact title:** Posters and flyers

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer knows the four-step printable
workflow: (1) say the real job first — what, when, where; (2) make the words
exact, in quotes; (3) split the job — AI draws a text-free picture, you add
the words; (4) run the print checklist (tall shape, dark on light, the
three-second test) and label the picture AI-made.

**Structure:** Three acts.

- Act 1 — The job and the companion (3 beats): the poster as a big piece of
  paper with three jobs (headline, details, picture); the sister film's
  ladder and five-slot recipe in one pointer beat; step one — name the real
  facts first (what, when, where).
- Act 2 — The workflow (4 beats): step two — exact words in quotes, because
  generators draw letters as shapes and misspell them; step three — ask for
  the layout (headline on top, details below, empty bottom half); the pro
  move — split the job (AI pictures, your words, added in any simple poster
  tool); the print checklist — tall shape, dark words on light paper, the
  three-second arm's-length test.
- Act 3 — The honest rule and the recap (2 beats): say it's AI-made — most
  picture apps offer an AI label, and honesty is the whole brand on a flyer;
  the four-step recap beat.

**Tone:** practical and plain-spoken; encouraging, never hypey. Teardown
register throughout — short sentences, every term explained on first use.
The film teaches a workflow (words + layout + print checks), not a product.

**What this film is not:** not a review of any image generator; no
version-specific features are asserted, so the film does not date; no pricing
tiers are quoted; not legal advice on AI disclosure rules. It does not show
real AI-generated images — every "result" on screen is a drawn Manim
placeholder in house style, deliberately generic.

**Source facts:**
- Prompt recipe and ladder (subject + setting + style + lighting + mood;
  start vague, iterate like texting a friend) are this film's companion
  source, taught in `pictures-from-words`. [record]
- Text-to-image models draw letters as shapes rather than typing them;
  misspelled words in generated images are a widely documented failure
  mode (PetaPixel, 2024; TechCrunch, 2024, citing UCL and DAIR
  researchers) — inherited from the companion film's fact-check. [record]
- Major platforms (YouTube, TikTok, Meta apps, X) carry AI-content labels;
  self-disclosure is the established norm; EU AI Act Article 50 requires
  visible labels on AI-generated content that could be mistaken for
  authentic — inherited from the companion film's fact-check. [record]
- Paper sizes: A4 (210 x 297 mm) and US Letter (8.5 x 11 in) are the
  standard portrait printable shapes; the film names them only as "the
  tall shape paper is" and never asserts a generator's output resolution.
