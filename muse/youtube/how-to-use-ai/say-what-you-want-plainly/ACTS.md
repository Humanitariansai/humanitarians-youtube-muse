# ACTS.md — Say what you want, plainly

A show-tell film for the humanitarians AI YouTube channel
(https://www.youtube.com/@humanitariansai): the single highest-leverage
prompting habit — be clear and direct. A vague prompt leaves blank lines
the AI fills with guesses; a brief says what you want, plainly.

**Exact title:** Say what you want, plainly

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer knows the one habit that most
improves AI answers — writing a brief instead of a wish — can name the
four questions a good brief answers (how long, what shape, who it's for,
what must be in it), and has a concrete rewrite to try on a prompt they
already use.

**Structure:** three acts.

- Act 1 — The problem (B00–B02): the prompt is the biggest lever on the
  answer; a vague prompt hands the AI blank lines it fills with guesses;
  those guesses come from the default — the most common answer, not yours.
- Act 2 — The fix (B03–B05): say what you want, plainly; the four
  questions every good brief answers; the new-hire test (a brilliant
  employee on day one knows nothing about your context, so tell them).
- Act 3 — The proof and the handoff (B06–B07, BHTF): a vague prompt
  rewritten into a brief, line by line; the same job side by side —
  vague gets three pages that fit nobody, the brief gets one page that
  fits; Your Turn hands the viewer the four-question audit for one of
  their own prompts.

**Tone:** Teardown at its plainest — short sentences, every term defined
in the same breath it appears ("a brief: a prompt that says exactly what
you want, like a work brief for a colleague"), no jargon left standing.
Liam's voice, warm and direct.

**What this film is not:** not a prompt-engineering course — it teaches
one habit, not a taxonomy. Not a product demo — no specific AI product is
shown or named beyond the handoff's "paste this into Claude." Not a
lecture about how AI works inside — the mechanism ("blanks become
guesses") is shown, not the machinery.
