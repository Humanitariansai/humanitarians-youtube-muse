# SHOTLIST.md — Say what you want, plainly

Scene → beat → what the viewer sees → claim it carries. All body beats
are drawn (Manim, show-tell style). The card test was run per beat: every
idea here is a thing, a part, or a flow (a slip, a block, pages, chips),
never an interface, a dataset, or a single number — so the film uses
**zero cards**, all drawings.

| Scene | Beat | What the viewer sees | Claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| `BrutalistHesitantWriter` | BIDEA | "Hallo. This is Liam, in for Bear." The writer types "Write me a summary of this", then corrects "write a summary" → "a three-sentence summary of this page, in plain words, for a teammate". | The film's thesis in one correction: vague wish → direct brief. |
| `ClaudeDefinitions` | BDEFS | "Terms In This Film": prompt / brief / default, one line each. | The three words the film needs, defined up front. |
| `B00_PromptSlip` | B00 | A kraft prompt slip drops onto the stage, lands with a shadow; label "prompt"; a terracotta dot pulses on it. | A prompt is the biggest lever on the answer — the words you put in. |
| `B01_Guesses` | B01 | A dark AI block; the short slip "write a summary" slides in; three mismatched pages pop out in a row, labelled "guesses". | Every blank line in a vague prompt is a guess the AI must make. |
| `B02_MostCommon` | B02 | The AI block with a row of small grey pages behind it ("most people"); the vague slip slides in; one grey page pops out ("most common answer"); a small slip labelled "you" sits apart and gets a terracotta dot. | Guesses come from the default — the most common answer, not yours. |

**Why no cards:** the card test needs (1) the idea to be an interface /
numbers / one word, (2) the card's motion to be the beat's claim, and
(3) the card to beat a drawing of the film's own cast. No body beat
passes (1): even B04's four questions are things (chips dropped onto the
film's own slip), drawn better than any card. The bookend composer and
definitions cards are the house ones, not body cards.
