## The argument (one paragraph)

AI makes a good buying co-pilot: it helps you choose without choosing for
you. The method: start with your own priorities (who it's for, how you'll use
it, what would make you regret it), ask for a side-by-side table with one row
per criterion, then ask the trade-off question — what would I be giving up
with each option. Don't trust star averages: some reviews are bought, some
are written by people who never bought the thing; ask the AI what the unhappy
buyers agree on — patterns, not stars. Paste the fine print and ask "what's
the catch": warranty coverage, return shipping, auto-renewals. One honest
limit: the AI doesn't know today's price — always click through and check the
live price yourself. You stay the decider; the AI does the reading.

## Deliberate anti-rot choices

- No product endorsements and no pricing tiers — the film teaches the
  comparison method, so no brand can go stale and no price can go wrong.
  The only product named is the generic "vacuum" in the naive question.
- The fine-print step says "paste the return policy, the warranty, the
  subscription terms" — document types, never a specific store's page.
- "The AI doesn't know today's price" is a structural claim about training
  cutoffs, not a version number — it stays true as models change.
- The Your-Turn prompt uses [A]/[B]/[X,Y,Z] placeholders so the viewer
  fills in their own purchase.
