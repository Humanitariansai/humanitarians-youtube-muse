# ACTS.md — Slides without the slog

A "How to AI" film (Wave 6, "Making things") for the humanitarians AI
YouTube channel. A practical habit film: turning rough notes into a real
slide deck in three moves — outline first, then slides, then polish —
instead of asking the AI for the whole deck in one prompt and getting
back walls of text. Companion to film 1 redo `the-long-game`: that film
keeps a long document from falling apart with outline → sections →
stitching; this one applies the same shape to decks (outline → slides →
polish), referenced once by name and never re-taught.

**Exact title:** Slides without the slog

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer knows why asking an AI for a
whole slide deck in one prompt returns walls of text — and how to replace
it with three moves: the outline (the one-line-per-slide skeleton you
read, fix, and approve before any slide exists), one slide per prompt
(each slide small enough to check), and the polish (cut the words off
the slides into the speaker notes, then run the back-row test).

**Structure:** Two acts.

- Act 1 — The one-prompt trap (3 beats): the goal (a pile of rough notes
  in, a five-slide deck out, every slide clean enough to read from the
  back row); the all-at-once ask (eighteen slides in about a minute, and
  every one a wall of text, because the AI did not know what mattered);
  why it happens (a slide is not a page — a full slide gets read instead
  of listened to; an AI building the whole deck at once guesses what
  matters, it cannot know what you would cut).
- Act 2 — The three moves (6 beats): move one, the outline
  (one-line-per-slide skeleton; you read it, fix it, approve it — the
  outline is the contract, same as in The long game); move two, one slide
  per prompt (slide one only, with the outline beside it, checked before
  slide two starts); move three, the polish (cut each slide to a
  headline plus a few short lines; the cut words move into the speaker
  notes, the lines only you see); the back-row test (step back from the
  screen: if you cannot catch the headline in a few seconds, cut again);
  the side-by-side (the one-prompt deck vs. outline → slides → polish);
  the habit (outline first, one slide per prompt, polish last).

**Tone:** plain and direct, Teardown register — short sentences, no
jargon left unexplained. "Speaker notes" gets its plain explanation in
the same breath ("the lines only you see"). The film teaches one habit,
not a technique. The voice explains; the drawings stay minimal (one cast
of objects the whole way: the notes page, the slide card, text lines
that get cut, the outline card, arrows, checks and flag dots).

**What this film is not:** not a prompt-engineering lecture; no
statistics about model performance are claimed (there are none to
cite — this is a behavioral film); no named models are recommended;
no guarantee that the method produces a perfect deck — the whole method
assumes every slide needs checking. No pricing tiers are quoted
(they churn too fast).
