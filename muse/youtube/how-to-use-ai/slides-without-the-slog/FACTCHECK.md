# FACTCHECK.md — Slides without the slog

This is a behavioral habit film: it teaches a working method, not facts
about a product. No statistics, benchmarks, or study results are claimed
anywhere in the script. Every checkable claim is a plain description of
ordinary AI-chat behavior or the film's own advice. No pricing tiers are
quoted (they churn too fast, per the standing rule).

## Verified / held (record)

1. The film claims that asking for a whole slide deck in one prompt
   tends to return slides packed with text — "every single one was a
   wall of text … the AI did not know what mattered. So it kept
   everything". → Stated as ordinary experience ("So he pasted…",
   "Here's why"), not as a measured result. No number is attached. [record]
2. The film's advice: outline first (one line per slide; read, fix, and
   approve the outline before any slide exists), build one slide per
   prompt against the outline, then polish (cut each slide to a
   headline plus a few short lines; move the cut words into the
   speaker notes; run the back-row test). → Presented as the film's
   method ("the three-move way", "the outline is the contract"), not as
   a universally proven optimum. [record]
3. Terms: "outline" = the skeleton of the deck, one line per slide;
   "slide" = one idea on one card, a headline not an essay; "polish" =
   cutting each slide down until it reads from the back row. → Plain-language
   working definitions for a general audience; standard usage. [record]
4. The meeting-notes scenario ("a friend of mine dumped his meeting
   notes") is the film's own dramatization of the series brief, chosen
   for the humanitarians audience. → Not presented as a real event; the
   BIDEA narration frames it as the film's storytelling, matching the
   companion film's "a colleague of mine" frame. [record]
5. The companion reference: B03 says "The outline is the contract, same
   as in The long game." → A single name reference to the companion
   film `the-long-game` (outline → sections → stitching); nothing from
   that film is re-taught — no outline mechanics are re-explained here.
   [record]
6. "Hallo" voices cleanly in Kokoro (show-tell skill, tested across the
   series). The narration avoids spelled-out acronyms, version numbers,
   and numbers spoken right before "Claude", per the skill's known
   misreading list. "Eighteen slides", "five-slide" are ordinary spoken
   words, not version numbers. [record]

## Judgments (judgment)

- "A slide is not a page" and "walls get read" are the film's own
  phrasing of the presentation-design commonplace that text-heavy
  slides pull the audience's attention off the speaker. [judgment]
- The wall-of-text cards, the audience sight lines, the shrinking
  back-row slide, and the side-by-side are dramatizations of the
  method, not screenshots of a real AI conversation. [judgment]
- "Speaker notes" is explained in the same breath it is introduced
  ("the speaker notes — the lines only you see"), per the general-audience
  rule that every term gets explained. [judgment]
- The outline card's slide rows, the notes card's lines, and the B08
  rule card's three lines are the documents' own content written on the
  card — a judgment call documented in SHOTLIST.md, not a
  label-inside-an-outline violation. [judgment]
- The film does not claim the method produces a perfect deck — only
  that a checked outline plus checked slides beats an uncheckable
  single-shot deck. [judgment]
- No "why now" statistic beat: the skill's spine allows it, but no
  honest number exists for this claim, so the takeaway beat (B08, the
  habit) takes its place — same call as the companion film. [judgment]

## Cut or disclosed

- No performance statistics of any kind are used — deliberately, per
  the task brief ("no invented statistics. Keep claims behavioral and
  modest").
- The film never promises the AI will be right; the whole method assumes
  every slide needs checking ("you read it before slide two starts").
