# ACTS — Tell the AI who to be

Film #2 of the How-to-AI series · show-tell · 11 beats · ~242 s estimated.

## Bookends (house show-tell)

- **BIDEA — The question.** Hesitant writer. Liam greets ("Hallo. This is Liam,
  in for Bear."), types the naive request ("write me a recipe for lemon pasta")
  and corrects it into a role prompt ("act as a chef and write me a recipe for
  lemon pasta"). The correction IS the film's subject.
- **BDEFS — Terms in this film.** role prompt / register / persona, one line each.

## Body

- **Act 1 — The trick (B00).** The hero object: one line at the start of a prompt,
  "act as a chef" — a role prompt. It doesn't change what you asked for; it
  changes who you're asking. The tagged card slides into Claude.
- **Act 2 — What it really is (B01–B02).** Not a costume: the AI doesn't become
  the chef. The role is four dials — words, assumed knowledge, tone, depth —
  which together are the *register*. B01 names the dials and snaps them to a
  calibrated setting. B02 runs the mechanism: the "patient tutor" role swings
  all four at once and the answer changes with no new facts added.
- **Act 3 — The experiment (B03).** The canonical case from the source lesson:
  one question, two doctors. The pediatric oncologist talking to a child vs the
  pathologist at grand rounds. Same facts, two answers; the role added no facts,
  it set the register.
- **Act 4 — Role vs persona (B04).** A role is small and functional (a name tag:
  "expert tax attorney") — it calibrates. A persona is the whole costume
  ("Marcus, a sardonic 1970s tax attorney…") — it adds character and voice.
  Both valid; know which you need.
- **Act 5 — When to use it (B05–B06).** The test: does the role change what
  counts as a good answer? Two piles — feedback/explaining/advice (role helps)
  vs extracting a date/doing a sum (skip it). Then the craft tip: specific
  beats vague; name the job and the audience ("patient math tutor for a
  ten-year-old", not "genius"); put the line first.

## Bookends (close)

  expert explaining to a curious beginner" vs "act as an expert explaining to a
  fellow expert" — and compare. Two self-checks.
