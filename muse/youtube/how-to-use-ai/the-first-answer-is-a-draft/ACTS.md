## The idea (one sentence)
Most people accept the AI's first answer as final; the power move is treating it as **draft one** and iterating — push back ("shorter", "more concrete", "try again but for a busy parent") and steer until the answer is yours.

## Audience
Smart, pragmatic general audience, not AI experts. Every term is explained in the same breath it is used ("pushback, which means your corrections — shorter, more concrete, try again").

## Acts
1. **The question** (BIDEA): hesitant writer — "Give me the perfect workout plan for my week" corrected to "…the first draft of a workout plan for my week". Liam: most people read the first answer and stop.
2. **Terms** (BDEFS): "first draft" (the AI's first try — rough, generic, meant to be rewritten), "pushback" (your corrections that turn the draft into the answer).
3. **The object** (B00): the draft page arrives, stamped DRAFT 1.
4. **The trap** (B01): most people treat the page like it's final — the FINAL stamp slams down too early.
5. **The demo, one page evolving** (B02–B05): "plan my dinners this week" → long vague draft one → pushback "shorter" (page trims) → pushback "more concrete" (taco night, soup, stir-fry) → steer "for a busy parent" (15-minute clocks).
6. **The payoff** (B06): draft one vs draft three side by side — the AI didn't get smarter, you steered it.
7. **The wobble** (B07): when pushback drifts off track, one line brings it back — you're the boss.
8. **The recipe** (B08): three pushbacks that do most of the work — shorter, more concrete, for someone.
9. **Your turn** (BHTF): composer prompt to run the three-pushback drill on the viewer's own task.
10. **Outro** (BOUT): "The first answer is a draft. At Nik Bear Brown."
