# ACTS.md — The long game

A "How to AI" film for the humanitarians AI YouTube channel. A practical
habit film: when a document is too long for one prompt, outline it first,
write it one section at a time, then stitch the sections together —
writing anything big without it falling apart. Companion to film 11,
`small-steps-big-jobs`: that film breaks a big job into steps; this one
keeps a long document from falling apart.

**Exact title:** The long game

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer knows why asking an AI to write
a whole long document in one prompt returns something that falls apart on
reading — and how to replace it with three moves: the outline (the
contract you approve before any prose), sections written one at a time
against the outline, and the stitching pass that makes them read as one
document.

**Structure:** Two acts.

- Act 1 — The one-prompt trap (3 beats): the goal (a twenty-page grant
  proposal that reads like one person wrote it); the all-at-once ask
  (twenty pages in thirty seconds — repeats, contradictions, wandering
  tone); why it drifts (nobody can keep twenty pages straight in one
  pass; a long document is five short ones stapled together).
- Act 2 — The three moves (6 beats): move one, the outline (the
  section-by-section skeleton; you read it, fix it, approve it — the
  outline is the contract); move two, one section at a time (section one
  written with the outline beside it, small enough to check); move three,
  the handoff (each section gets the outline plus the last section, so
  page fourteen keeps agreeing with page three); the stitching (read end
  to end, fix only the seams); the side-by-side (the one-prompt version
  vs. outline → sections → stitching); the habit (outline first, one
  section per prompt, stitch at the end).

**Tone:** plain and direct, Teardown register — short sentences, no
jargon left unexplained. The film teaches one habit, not a technique.
The voice explains; the drawings stay minimal (one cast of objects the
whole way: the page stack, the outline card, section pages, arrows, a
scan line, checks and flag dots).

**What this film is not:** not a prompt-engineering lecture; no
statistics about model performance are claimed (there are none to
cite — this is a behavioral film); no named models are recommended;
no guarantee that the method produces a perfect document — the whole
method assumes every section needs checking.
