# FACTCHECK.md — The long game

This is a behavioral habit film: it teaches a working method, not facts
about a product. No statistics, benchmarks, or study results are claimed
anywhere in the script. Every checkable claim is a plain description of
ordinary AI-chat behavior or the film's own advice.

## Verified / held (record)

1. The film claims that asking for a whole long document in one prompt
   tends to return output that repeats itself, contradicts itself, and
   shifts tone — "the middle repeated the beginning, page twelve
   contradicted page three, and the tone wandered off". → Stated as
   ordinary experience ("So she asked…", "Here's why"), not as a
   measured result. No number is attached. [record]
2. The film's advice: outline first (and approve the outline before any
   prose), write one section at a time against the outline, hand each
   section the outline plus the previous section, then do a stitching
   pass that fixes only the seams. → Presented as the film's method
   ("the three-part method", "the outline is the contract"), not as a
   universally proven optimum. [record]
3. Terms: "outline" = the skeleton of the document, every part in
   order; "section" = one chapter's worth of the document; "stitch" =
   reading it as one piece and fixing the seams. → Plain-language
   working definitions for a general audience; standard usage. [record]
4. The twenty-page grant proposal (community food bank) is the film's
   own dramatization of the series brief, chosen for the humanitarians
   audience. → Not presented as a real event; the BIDEA narration says
   "a colleague of mine" as the film's storytelling frame, matching the
   companion film's "a friend of mine" frame. [record]
5. "Hallo" voices cleanly in Kokoro (show-tell skill, tested across the
   series). The narration avoids spelled-out acronyms, version numbers,
   and numbers spoken right before "Claude", per the skill's known
   misreading list. "Twenty-page", "thirty seconds", "page fourteen"
   are ordinary spoken words, not version numbers. [record]

## Judgments (judgment)

- "A long document is really five short ones stapled together" is the
  film's own metaphor for document decomposition, echoing the companion
  film's "five small jobs wearing a trench coat". [judgment]
- The scattered pages, the drifting page numbers, and the side-by-side
  are dramatizations of the method, not screenshots of a real AI
  conversation. [judgment]
- The outline card's section rows and the B08 rule card's three lines
  are the documents' own content written on the card — a judgment call
  documented in SHOTLIST.md, not a label-inside-an-outline violation.
  [judgment]
- The film does not claim the method produces a perfect document — only
  that a checked outline plus checked sections beats an uncheckable
  single-shot document. [judgment]
- No "why now" statistic beat: the skill's spine allows it, but no
  honest number exists for this claim, so the takeaway beat (B08, the
  habit) takes its place — same call as the companion film. [judgment]

## Cut or disclosed

- No performance statistics of any kind are used — deliberately, per the
  task brief ("no invented statistics. Keep claims behavioral and
  modest").
- The film never promises the AI will be right; the whole method assumes
  every section needs checking ("Small enough to check in one sitting",
  "you read it before the next one starts").
