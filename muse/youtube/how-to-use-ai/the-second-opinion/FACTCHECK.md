# FACTCHECK.md — "The Second Opinion"

Checked 2026-10-04. Rule: no invented statistics, modest claims only.
"Two opinions beat one" is argued as practical advice, never quantified.

| # | Claim (as the film states it) | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Steel-manning means arguing the other side's case at its strongest, fairly stated — the opposite of a straw man. | CONFIRMED | "A steel man is the opposite, of course, of a straw man... the art of addressing the best form of the other person's argument" (Reason/Volokh Conspiracy, 2021); the term gained traction in the rationalist movement; Daniel Dennett's first rule of a good debate is to state the target's position so fairly they say "I wish I thought of putting it that way" (Big Think, summarizing Dennett's Intuition Pumps). See SOURCES.md. [record] |
| 2 | A devil's advocate is someone whose job is to argue against the plan. | CONFIRMED | The advocatus diaboli (Promoter of the Faith) was a real office in the Catholic Church: appointed to argue against a candidate's canonization, "to look for holes in the evidence" (Wikipedia); a former office formally established under Sixtus V in 1587 (Britannica). The film uses the modern transferred sense, which the sources confirm is the standard idiom. See SOURCES.md. [record] |
| 3 | Two AIs can share the same blind spots because they train on similar data. | JUDGMENT, consistent with sources | Models trained on overlapping web-scale corpora share many of the same biases and failure modes; this is uncontroversial in the field and is the reason ensemble diversity matters. The film states it as a caution ("can share"), not a measured rate. [judgment] |
| 4 | One AI arguing against itself still runs on the same brain. | CONFIRMED BY REASONING | A self-debate is generated by the same model weights; nothing external corrects it. Stated as a structural fact about how the move works, not as an empirical claim. [record] |
| 5 | "Do you agree?" gets a nod; flaw-hunting prompts ("steelman the opposite case", "what would change your answer?") get real work. | JUDGMENT, consistent with sources | Prompt-engineering guidance warns that vague prompts ("make it better") underperform specific ones with a concrete role and goal (reviewaitool.com 2026; mendbot best-practices). The film extends this to agreement-seeking vs steelmanning as practical advice, not a lab result. [judgment] |
| 6 | Keeping the second take independent (don't show it the first answer first) avoids polite agreement. | JUDGMENT | Sequential prompting can anchor a model on the earlier answer; asking independently is standard good practice in the multi-agent debate literature (independent first-round answers before debate rounds). Framed as advice, not a measured effect. [judgment] |
| 7 | No accuracy-improvement statistic is stated. | CONFIRMED BY INSPECTION | The narration and on-screen text contain no percentages, multipliers, or study citations. The multi-agent debate paper's measured gains are deliberately NOT quoted in the film — they date fast and the film's audience needs the habit, not the numbers. [record] |
| 8 | A medical question should still go to a real doctor. | JUDGMENT (safety policy of the channel) | The film names "medical question" among high-stakes decisions and explicitly says to take it to a professional — this is a deliberate guardrail, not medical advice. [judgment] |

drifting count. The technique is interface-agnostic (works in Claude,
ChatGPT, Gemini, and others — the film names none in the technique beats).
