# ACTS.md — Trust, but verify

Film 21 of the "How to AI" series (humanitarians channel Muse series). A
general-audience ai-explainer: how to check AI claims before you act on
them. REFACTOR of
`fellows/aujaswi-a/2026-08-19-how-to-spot-unreliable-ai-research`
(nikbearbrown/humanitarians-youtube-muse) — generalized from spotting
unreliable AI *research* to a verification habit for *any* AI claim that
matters, rewritten for a smart non-expert audience.

**Exact title:** Trust, but verify

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer owns a 60-second, four-step
verification habit for any AI claim that matters: (1) ask how sure it is
and what would change its mind, (2) ask for the source and actually open
it, (3) cross-check with one independent search, (4) for numbers, make it
show its work. Plus the calibration rule: match the checking effort to
the stakes.

**Structure:** Three acts, 10 beats, ~266 s (~4.5 min).

- Act 1 — The hook and the vocabulary (2 beats): an AI states the
  "10% of your brain" myth with total confidence and is completely
  wrong — the reason a habit beats hope. Five plain-language terms:
  hallucination, confident tone, primary source, independent check,
  stakes.
- Act 2 — The 60-second habit (4 beats): one beat per step, each with a
  visual the viewer watches happen. Step 2 carries the centerpiece demo:
  a plausible, footnoted sleep claim evaporates the moment its source
  link is actually opened.
- Act 3 — Calibration and handoff (4 beats): not every claim earns the
  full habit — match effort to stakes; the one-card recap (the film's
  verdict); a paste-ready "Your turn" prompt for the viewer's own AI
  app; the title-restate outro.

**Tone:** Teardown register — plain sentences, no unexplained jargon,
show rather than tell. The AI is a brilliant intern with no shame; the
viewer stays the editor. No scolding, no paranoia: the habit is cheap,
fast, and empowering.

**What this film is not:** not a lecture on AI safety or research
methods; not a list of "AI lies" gotchas; not advice for any specific
medical, legal, or financial decision (the film teaches *how to check*,
never what the answer is). It does not claim AI is untrustworthy — it
claims confidence is not evidence.

**Source facts:**
- "10% of the brain" is a neuromyth: fMRI and a century of
  neuroscience show we use virtually every part of the brain; CNRS
  calls the claim "completely false". (search-grounded, FACTCHECK.md)
- LLMs confabulate: they generate fluent, confident text that can be
  false, with invented citations — the well-documented "hallucination"
  behavior of large language models. (general documented behavior)
- Demo claim (banana/40%/Journal of Sleep Medicine): a *constructed
  illustration* of a hallucinated AI answer, written for this film —
  presented on screen as the AI's answer being debunked, never endorsed
  as true. (FACTCHECK.md)
