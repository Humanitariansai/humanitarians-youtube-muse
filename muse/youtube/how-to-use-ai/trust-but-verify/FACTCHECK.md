# FACTCHECK.md — Trust, but verify

Every claim in the script was checked before writing. Unverifiable claims
were cut; hedges are kept where the claim is one person's framing.
Values labeled [record] / [judgment] / [my input].

## Verified (record)

1. The "humans only use 10% of their brains" claim is false. CNRS calls it
   "completely false — and nothing more than a neuromyth"; neurologist
   Barry Gordon (Johns Hopkins, via Scientific American) says the myth is
   "so wrong it is almost laughable"; fMRI/PET imaging shows we use
   virtually every part of the brain over time. The script's line "it's
   completely false" is supported. [record]
   (https://news.cnrs.fr/opinions/debunking-neuromyths ;
   https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/do-people-only-use-10-percent-of-their-brains/)

2. Large language models hallucinate: they produce fluent, confident-sounding
   text — including invented facts and citations — that is not grounded in
   any source. This is the standard, widely documented "hallucination" /
   confabulation behavior of LLMs; the script's plain-language definition
   ("the AI inventing something that sounds true — not lying, inventing")
   matches the documented behavior. [record]

## Constructed for the film (my input — not a factual claim)

3. The B02 demo answer — "A 2023 study in the Journal of Sleep Medicine
   found that a banana before bed helps you fall asleep 40% faster" — is a

   this film. It is presented on screen and in narration strictly as *the
   AI's answer being debunked*, never endorsed as true. No real study,
   journal issue, or 40% figure is asserted; the beat's whole point is that
