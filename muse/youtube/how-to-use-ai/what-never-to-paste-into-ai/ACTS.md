# ACTS.md — What never to paste into AI.

A privacy rules-of-thumb film for the humanitarians channel "How to AI"
series: the one memorable rule (the postcard test), the concrete never-paste
list, and the positive side — how to still get help. Companion to
`personal-ai-at-work` (same series folder): that film covers work
confidentiality on personal accounts (the Samsung story, training toggles,
the clean-room rule). This film must not contradict it — it doesn't: the
training/opt-out mechanism here is the same, and the B06 narration points
at the work film for the full version rather than re-teaching it.

**Exact title:** What never to paste into AI.

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer can state the postcard test in one
sentence, name the four things that never go into a chatbot (passwords and
credentials; full money numbers; other people's private info; work secrets
they don't own), explain in plain words why pasted text can end up stored,
reviewed, or used for training, and redact or anonymize before pasting.

**Skill:** lecture. cc-explainer was assigned and rejected: cc-explainer is
for real terminal sessions — this film has no terminal. The lecture spine
(BIDEA hesitant writer → BDEFS key terms → acts → BVDT recap → BHTF
your-turn → BOUT) is the package convention of the sibling film, and
lecture's BDEFS beat is what the audience rule needs (every technical term
defined up front, in plain language).

**Structure:** Three acts.

- Act 1 — Why pasting is a postcard (2 beats): the journey your text takes
  (your phone → the company's servers → possibly stored, reviewed by staff,
  or used for training; "not will — can"); settings change the answer
  (personal plans: find the training toggle; business plans: no training by
  default; defaults change, so check).
- Act 2 — The never list (4 beats): passwords and credentials; full money
  numbers (balances are facts, account numbers are keys); other people's
  private info (kids' names/schools, medical details, someone else's
  messages — you can't consent for them); work-confidential material you
  don't own (one-line nod to the companion film's argument).
- Act 3 — Get help anyway (3 beats): redact (black bars; the AI answers
  anyway); anonymize with placeholders ("my client — call them Client X";
  describe instead of paste); the 3-second postcard reflex before you hit
  enter.

**Tone:** calm and practical, Teardown register throughout — plain words,
no fear-mongering, no timeless claims about company policies. A rule you
can remember, not a scare.

**What this film is not:** not a claim that AI chatbots are unsafe to use;
not legal advice; not a repetition of the work film (it points there, once,
in one sentence); not a how-to for any one product's settings — toggle
paths and policy details are deliberately phrased evergreen ("check yours").

**Recurring cast (one look per act):** the chat bubble and the postcard with
its stamp (Act 1); the ACCENT cross-out bars and the INK ban sign (Act 2);
the document plate and the BLUE placeholder tags (Act 3).

**Source facts:**
- What you paste leaves your device and lands on the AI company's servers;
  depending on the product and its settings it can be stored, reviewed by
  staff (safety/abuse review; contractors reviewing chats for model
  improvement — 404 Media, Sep 2026), or used to train future models.
  (Anthropic consumer terms via 2026 guides; OpenAI FAQ: authorized
  personnel may view user data to improve model performance; Google notes
  humans may review saved Gemini chats)
- Consumer-plan training is opt-out on the major chatbots; the toggle lives
  in settings (exact wording changes — the film says "find the training
  toggle"). Opted-in chats can be kept for years (Anthropic: up to 5 years
  in training pipelines; opted out: ~30-day deletion). Business/Enterprise
  plans don't train on your data by default. (verified 2026-10-03)
