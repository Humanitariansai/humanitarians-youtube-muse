# FACTCHECK.md — What never to paste into AI.

Every checkable claim was verified on 2026-10-03, before the script was
written. Company policies change; the narration is deliberately phrased
evergreen ("depending on the product and its settings", "find the training
toggle", "defaults change — check yours") so the film degrades gracefully.
No statistics are invented; there are no statistics in the film.

## Verified (record)

1. Pasted chat text leaves the user's device and is processed on the AI
   company's servers; companies may store conversations, and human staff or
   contractors may review them — for safety/abuse enforcement and for model
   improvement. → OpenAI FAQ (authorized personnel and service providers
   may view user data to improve model performance; advises against entering
   sensitive information — notice online since 2023); 404 Media reporting
   Sep 2026 (contractors reviewing real ChatGPT chats for model
   improvement, via a privacy filter that can miss identifiers); Anthropic
   confirmed to 404 Media it uses human reviewers on Claude responses for
   users with training enabled; Google notes humans may review saved Gemini
   chats. [record]
2. Consumer-plan training is opt-out on the major chatbots. Anthropic
   (Free/Pro/Max): training on by default since Sep 2025 policy change;
   toggle in Settings → Privacy (exact wording has changed over time —
   "You can help improve Claude" / "Help improve our AI models"); opting in
   extends retention up to 5 years in training pipelines; opted out,
   ~30-day deletion. OpenAI: "Improve the model for everyone" on by default
   for Free/Plus/Pro; off by default for Enterprise/Business/Edu.
   Anthropic Team/Enterprise/API: no training on customer data.
   → guides verified 2026-10-03 (anarlog, llmnesia, theairankings, 404 Media
   via the-decoder). [record]
3. Opting out applies going forward; data already used in training cannot
   be pulled back. → Anthropic's own announcement and the 2026 guides.
   The film does not state this (it says "check yours" instead) — noted
   here so a future revision can add it. [record]
4. "Never paste" categories are the film's own rules of thumb, not vendor
   policy: (a) credentials — whoever holds them owns the account;
   (b) full financial account numbers — the digits that move money;
   (c) other people's private info — you cannot consent for them;
   (d) work-confidential material you don't own. No citation needed; these
   are Liam's judgments, presented as such. [judgment]
5. The postcard test is the film's own framing of a common infosec rule of
   thumb ("would you put it on a postcard?"). Not attributed to any source.
   [judgment]
6. All on-screen example data is obviously fake and clearly marked as
   example: 4111 1111 1111 1111 (the standard test card number),
   "pa55word-EXAMPLE", "sk-fake-0000", "routing: 000000000",
   "acct: 0000-0000", fictional "Acme Corp". No real credentials, no real
   people, no real account numbers appear anywhere. [record]
7. Companion consistency: `personal-ai-at-work` teaches that personal-plan
   chats can train the model (opt-out), business plans don't train by
   default, and the clean-room rule (anonymize before you paste). This film
   repeats none of its specifics, points at it once in B06 ("a whole film
   on the work version of this rule"), and contradicts nothing. [record]
