# ACTS.md — When it's confidently wrong.

An ai-explainer concept film for the humanitarians AI YouTube channel:
why the AI doubles down when it's wrong, and the four-step recovery
playbook — restart, ask for sources, narrow the question, verify
elsewhere.

**Exact title:** When it's confidently wrong.

**Core promise:** By the end, the viewer understands that the AI's
"confidence" is fluency, not knowledge — which is why arguing with it
fails — and can run the four-step recovery playbook the next time the AI
insists on something wrong.

**Structure:** Three acts.

- Act 1 — The problem (3 beats): a confident wrong answer lands in a
  chat (hallucination, defined in the same breath); the viewer challenges
  it and the AI doubles down; the mechanism in plain language — the
  model writes by picking the most likely next word, has no fact-checker
  and no honesty meter, and the most consistent reply to "I was right"
  is "I'm still right," so arguing feeds the loop.
- Act 2 — The recovery playbook (4 beats): step one, don't argue —
  restart in a fresh chat with new framing; step two, ask for sources
  and actually check one; step three, narrow the question until the
  error is cornered between small checkable facts; step four, know when
  to stop and verify elsewhere (search, a book, a person).
- Act 3 — Putting it to work (1 beat): a compressed worked example —
  the whole playbook in one pass against the film's staged exchange,
  ending in one recovered truth.

**Tone:** Teardown at its plainest — short sentences, every term defined
in the same breath it appears, no jargon left standing. Liam's voice,
warm and direct.

**What this film is not:** not a lesson in machine-learning theory —
"next-word prediction" is shown as word chips, never as math or
architecture. Not a claim about any specific AI product — the chat is a
drawn window, never a screenshot of a real interface. Not a promise
that the playbook always works — step four exists precisely because
sometimes it doesn't.
