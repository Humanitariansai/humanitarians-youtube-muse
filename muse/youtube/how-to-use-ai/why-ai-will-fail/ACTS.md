## The one insight

For over a century, credentialed experts have confidently declared new
technologies dead — and been wrong nearly every time. The mechanism is the
**insider trap**: the better you know the current game, the worse you are at
seeing the next one. Today's loudest version is "AI will fail." Judge the
trajectory, not the snapshot — and the bet is asymmetric: learning is cheap,
dismissing is expensive.

The whole film in one breath: a century-long pattern of confident expert

### ACT I — A century of wrong calls (B02–B05)
The evidence. B02 runs the list fast (Olsen 1977, Metcalfe 1995, Ballmer
2007, Krugman 1998) as quote→outcome cards. B03 lingers on the best
storytelling case (Keyes/Blockbuster vs Netflix). B04 pairs two more
(Ellison/cloud, Valenti/VCR) with their ironic outcomes. B05 widens the
frame beyond business: the BMJ on penicillin — even the gatekeepers of
science got the century's most important drug wrong in their own journal.

### ACT II — Why smart people get it wrong (B06–B07)
The mechanism, shown not told. B06 draws the insider trap: the incumbent
optimizes the old axis while the newcomer enters on a new one the old
scoreboard cannot see. B07 is the worked example: the toy curve. Skeptics
had real evidence (early streaming WAS a buffering mess) — they measured a
snapshot, not a trajectory.

### ACT III — The AI skeptics (B08–B09)
The pattern applied to the present. B08 wires each current claim to its
historical twin (hallucination ↔ pixel soup; productivity paradox ↔ Solow
1987; Acemoglu's estimate ↔ snapshot risk). B09 is the honest Teardown
beat: the skeptics describe the present accurately — the question is
whether you're judging a trajectory by a snapshot.

### ACT IV — The asymmetric bet (B10)
The practical payoff: a 2×2 matrix. Three of four futures reward learning;
the downside of learning is small, the downside of dismissing is large.

### Verdict — B11
Four-line recap: the century of wrong calls → the insider trap → trajectory
over snapshot → the asymmetric bet. Closes with the one question to carry:
"Am I scoring the new thing on the old game's scoreboard?"
